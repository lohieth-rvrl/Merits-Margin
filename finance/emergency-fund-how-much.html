Emergency Fund: How Much Do You Actually Need?

6 min read · Published 2026-05-18 · By the Ledger & Route editorial team

The standard "3 to 6 months" advice isn't wrong, but it's incomplete. Here's how to size yours.

Ad slot — in-article (responsive)

Why the generic range exists

The 3-to-6-months rule comes from a simple idea: if you lost your income today, how long could you cover essential costs before things got desperate? Three to six months is roughly how long it takes most people to find new work in a normal job market.

What should actually change your number

Your real target depends on factors the generic rule ignores:

  • Job stability -- commission-based or contract work pushes you toward the higher end, or beyond it
  • Number of income earners in your household -- a second income lowers the pressure on any single fund
  • Fixed obligations -- dependents, medical needs, or a mortgage raise your floor
  • How fast you could realistically re-earn income in your specific field

Where to actually keep it

An emergency fund needs to be boring and liquid: a high-yield savings account, not a brokerage account. The goal isn't growth, it's availability -- you want zero delay and zero risk of loss the day you actually need it.

Start smaller if 3-6 months feels impossible

If a full fund feels out of reach, a starter target of $1,000-$2,000 still covers most common emergencies -- a car repair, a medical copay, a broken appliance -- and prevents new debt while you build toward the larger number.
