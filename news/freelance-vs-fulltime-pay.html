Freelance vs. Full-Time: Which Actually Pays Better Right Now

6 min read · Published 2026-06-15 · By the Ledger & Route editorial team

A fair comparison has to account for benefits, taxes, and time -- not just the headline hourly rate.

Ad slot — in-article (responsive)

Why the raw hourly comparison is misleading

A freelance rate of $60/hour looks far better than a full-time salary that works out to $35/hour -- until you account for the costs a salary quietly absorbs: employer-subsidized health insurance, paid time off, retirement matching, and the employer's half of payroll taxes.

What to actually add to a freelance rate

A reasonable rule of thumb: a freelancer needs to charge roughly 1.5-2x an equivalent full-time hourly wage to match the total value of a full-time role, once benefits, self-employment tax, and unpaid non-billable time (marketing, admin, gaps between clients) are factored in.

Where freelancing genuinely wins

Independent of hourly math, freelancing wins on schedule control and, for people who can keep a full pipeline, a higher earnings ceiling than a fixed salary allows. It also carries more income volatility -- which is exactly why an irregular-income budgeting system matters if you go this route.
