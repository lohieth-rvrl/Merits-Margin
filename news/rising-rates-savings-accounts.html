What Rising Interest Rates Actually Mean for Your Savings Account

5 min read · Published 2026-06-25 · By the Ledger & Route editorial team

Higher rates aren't only bad news -- here's the side of the story that gets less coverage.

Ad slot — in-article (responsive)

The part that gets the headlines

Coverage of rate changes usually focuses on borrowers -- higher mortgage rates, pricier auto loans, tighter credit. That's a real and important effect. But it's only half the picture.

The part that doesn't

Higher rates typically mean better yields on savings accounts and CDs, sometimes meaningfully so. A savings account that paid close to nothing during a low-rate period can suddenly become a genuinely useful place to hold an emergency fund.

What to actually do about it

If your savings sit in an account that hasn't adjusted its rate, it's worth checking what competitive high-yield accounts are currently offering -- the gap between a stagnant account and a competitive one can be significant, and moving cash between them is usually free and immediate.
