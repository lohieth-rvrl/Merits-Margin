## Start with your floor, not your average

Most budgeting advice assumes a steady paycheck. If your income moves month to month, averaging it out is the wrong starting point -- an average hides the bad months, and the bad months are exactly when a budget needs to hold up.

Instead, look back over the last 6-12 months and find your **lowest** earning month. That number is your floor. Every essential expense -- rent, utilities, groceries, minimum debt payments -- has to fit inside that floor. If it doesn't, the fix is to cut the expense, not to hope for a better month.

## Use percentages, not fixed dollar amounts

Once your essentials fit inside your floor, split anything above it by percentage rather than a flat number, so the split automatically scales with a good month or a lean one:

- **50%** to essentials and near-term bills
- **20%** to savings and debt payoff beyond the minimum
- **20%** to a buffer account that smooths out next month
- **10%** to discretionary spending

## Build a one-month buffer before anything else

Before investing or aggressively paying down debt, put one full month of your floor expenses into a separate account you don't touch. This buffer is what lets you pay yourself a steady "salary" from irregular income -- you pull from the buffer in lean months and refill it in strong ones, so your day-to-day spending never has to react to a single bad month.

## Review monthly, not daily

Irregular income tempts people into checking their balance constantly. Instead, set one review day a month: log what came in, refill the buffer if it dipped, and adjust the next month's percentages if your floor has genuinely changed.
