## Lead with a skills summary, not a work history gap

If your work history section would be thin, don't lead with it. Open with a 3-4 line summary that states the role you want and the concrete skills you bring to it, so a hiring manager reads your strengths before they notice what's missing.

## Reframe projects and coursework as accomplishments

Instead of listing a class or project by name, describe what you built and the result, the same way you'd describe a job: "Built a budgeting app used by 40 classmates" reads like real experience, even without a job title attached to it.

## Volunteer and informal work counts

Running a club, organizing an event, managing a group project, or freelancing informally are all legitimate resume content. What matters is the responsibility you held and the outcome, not whether you were paid for it.

## Use the job posting's own language

Pull key phrases directly from the job description and mirror them in your skills and summary sections. Many companies scan resumes with software before a person ever reads them, and matching language improves your odds of clearing that first pass.
