## Never give a number first if you can avoid it

If asked your salary expectations early, it's fair to redirect: "I'd like to learn more about the role's scope first -- what range has the team budgeted for this position?" This keeps you from anchoring yourself too low before you understand the job fully.

## Responding to an offer below your target

A simple, direct script: "Thank you for the offer -- I'm genuinely excited about this role. Based on my research and experience, I was expecting something closer to [number]. Is there flexibility here?" This is polite, specific, and doesn't over-explain or apologize.

## Sitting with silence

After you state your number, stop talking. Silence feels uncomfortable, but it's doing work -- it gives the other side room to respond instead of you talking yourself back down.

## Negotiating beyond base salary

If the base number truly can't move, other levers are often more flexible: signing bonus, extra vacation days, a title adjustment, or an earlier performance review date. Naming these directly -- "If the base is fixed, is there room on [X] instead?" -- often unlocks something.
